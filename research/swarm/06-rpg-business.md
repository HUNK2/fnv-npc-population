# 06 - Business and base systems inside RPGs and open-world games

Agent angle: how side businesses and bases inside action RPGs keep a player engaged while they are off adventuring.
Gathered 2026-11-26. [D] = documented by the source (wiki, guide, developer, review stating a fact). [O] = player or
reviewer opinion. [M] = from my own memory of the game, not re-checked on a page this session (treat as unverified).
Honesty note: Fandom wikis returned HTTP 402, several outlets returned 403, and PC Gamer and GamesRadar pages came back
as navigation only. Where a page did not load I used the search-result summary of it and say so. Wikipedia pages for
AC4 and Witcher 3 DLC had nothing on the side systems (they were checked and are not cited for them).

## 1. Key findings

### Per game

**Assassin's Creed IV, Kenway's Fleet**
- Captured ships go into a fleet; you send them with supplies on trade routes to the Caribbean, the Americas, Europe
  and Africa. Success depends on ship capacity and resource management. Routes are colour-coded by danger: green safe,
  yellow hazardous, orange dangerous, red treacherous; dangerous routes are guarded by stronger enemies, and a turn-based
  naval fight (ship class matters) lowers a route's risk. [D] (gamepressure.com/assassinscreediv/kenways-fleet/z89672;
  search summary of gameskinny, supercheats)
- It is optional and not needed for the story. [D] Reception split: one reviewer found it "boring", another said it feels
  "perfectly woven into Kenway's pirate empire" and funds upgrades. [O] (search summary of Citizen, Softpedia, GamesBeat)
- Lesson: the idea (route colour = risk, a fight fixes the risk) is strong. The weak part was that it is a list of
  timed sends with little consequence for you personally. [O]

**AC II Monteriggioni and AC Brotherhood Rome**
- The villa generates florins over time into a chest that "must be collected regularly"; upgrades give shop discounts and
  raise income; buying upgrades and paintings raises the villa's worth. [D] (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassin%27s_Creed_II)
- Five shops, three renovation levels each; reward is discounts, new items, more money. [D] (search summary of AC wiki
  mirrors)
- Brotherhood scales it up: Rome is twelve districts, each held by a Borgia tower. You kill the captain and burn the
  tower first, then you may renovate shops (blacksmith, bank, tailor). Banks pool income so you stop touring chests. Total
  full-price renovation about 577,125 florins. [D] (theregister.com/2010/11/23/assassins_creed_brotherhood_1/?page=2;
  primagames.com and gamepressure.com via search)
- The design key is the gate: the main-game action (clear the tower) unlocks the right to invest. The investment then
  rebuilds the street you fought for. [D] (same sources). That is exactly the "deed + money" gate in our tier plan.

**Yakuza 0**
- Majima's Cabaret Club Czar is praised as "addictive" and "the best part" of the game; real estate (Kiryu) was found
  skippable by at least one reviewer. [O] (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakuza_0; pcgamer.com/yakuza-0-review via search)
- The cabaret is an active game: rush around doing small tasks for your hostesses so customers stay engaged; rival club
  operators (the Five Stars of Sotenbori) are the competition. [D/O] (search summary of PC Gamer, Prima)
- Real estate: rival "Five Billionaires" fight for land; income needs real time to pass; the shakedown/defend loop is the
  action part. [D/O] (search summary; I did not read the articles themselves, the PC Gamer hostess-club page loaded
  headline-only)
- Story hook: Majima runs the club as punishment for the 1985 assassination. The business is the character's place in
  the plot, not a detached menu. [D] (Wikipedia)
- Lesson: the business that works is the one with named staff you care about, a rival with a face, and an active mode
  where your own skill changes the result.

**Red Dead Redemption 2 camp and Red Dead Online Trader/Moonshiner**
- RDR2 camp: the gang's funds come from a donation box (and members donate; lodging upgrades raise donations); upgrades
  are bought through Strauss's ledger; wagons change what is stocked; cosmetics are crafted through Pearson. Camp moves
  each chapter but purchases persist. [D] (rdr2.org/guides/camp-upgrades-guide/)
- A guide-writer called donations "dumb" but upgrades fine. [O] (TechCrunch via search)
- RDO Trader: roles are designed to progress "more slowly than GTA Online's missions"; at launch players said rewards
  were too low for goods and upgrades (one estimate eight hours per gold bar) and Rockstar rebalanced. Moonshiners was
  praised by Eurogamer and Kotaku; the later Naturalist role was called too like Trader. [D/O]
  (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Dead_Online)
- Lesson: camp upgrades work because staff characters (Pearson, Strauss) are the shopfronts. A new role that is a copy
  of the old one is disliked.

**Witcher 3: Blood and Wine, Corvo Bianco**
- Upgrades are bought from a majordomo with crowns and finish in game days: general renovation 5,000 crowns / 3 days;
  armorer table 1,000 / 1 day; grindstone 1,000 / 1 day; bed 1,000 / 1 day (+1,000 vitality 120 min); stable 2,000 / 2
  days (Roach +100% durability 60 min); garden 2,000 / 2 days (rare herbs). After the first set the basement wall opens to
  a hidden alchemy lab; at the end the majordomo toasts with wine. [D] (gamepressure.com/thewitcher3bloodandwine/corvo-bianco/zb8e95)
- Who visits the estate depends on your earlier choices (Yennefer or Triss moves in, Ciri visits, Dandelion drops by).
  [D] (Wikipedia, Blood and Wine)
- Reviewers loved it as a favourite spot. [O] (everyeye.it via search)
- Lesson: every upgrade is one clear benefit you use immediately, takes game days (so time away is productive), and the
  base has a personality (the majordomo, the toast, the visiting character).

**Fallout 4 settlements**
- Complaints from a player thread: more time "tweaking and repairing" settlements than questing; compared to The Sims;
  attacks "every game day or so"; defences fail while you are away ("defense doesn't give any actual protection unless
  you are there"); attacks that never end; fixed enemy spawn points make walls pointless; tedious manual wall building;
  the attack triggers are unclear. [O] (steamcommunity.com/app/377160/discussions/0/492379439685339888)
- A developer said they are optional; others avoid them entirely. [O] (same thread)
- From search summaries: "constant upkeep", "feels like The Sims, not a post-nuclear RPG", settlers touching your items,
  repetitive "settlement needs help" quests, and attack notifications that interrupt. [O] (techraptor.net piece and Nexus
  forum posts, via search; the techraptor page itself returned 403)
- Lesson: the failure was not "building", it was (1) unclear rules, (2) away-time punished by random real-time attacks,
  (3) scale of upkeep growing with success, (4) the reward for defending being nothing.

**Fallout 76 vending**
- You build up to four vending machines at your CAMP, stock them, set prices; other players buy with caps; a 10% tax was
  added because caps entered the world with no sink; cap cap was 25,000 at launch (reported 40,000 later). [D/O]
  (search summary of GamesRadar, TechSpot, PC Gamer; articles loaded headline-only)
- Lesson: a currency sink must be designed in from day one; "tax to fix inflation" announced later felt like a penalty.

**Skyrim Hearthfire**
- Satisfying, "sense of progress", steward sells materials; but layouts are limited so homes look alike, costs mount, you
  gather your own iron, few quests; "building the rest feels like manual labor"; released too late to matter. [O]
  (gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2012/09/05/the-good-and-bad-of-skyrim-39-s-hearthfire-dlc.aspx; Escapist, Giant Bomb
  via search)
- Lesson: individuality (my base looks like mine) and no material-hauling chores.

**Dragon Age: Inquisition war table**
- Kotaku's writer praises it: small operations are hands-off, resolve after a time with a small reward, unlock after story
  missions, and make the world feel "fully alive"; advisors' commentary gives responsibility. Only drawback: around the
  midpoint "the War Table gets overwhelming" with more operations than you can finish. [O]
  (kotaku.com/dragon-age-inquisition-s-war-table-brought-the-wider-w-1823169693)
- Criticisms: timers in real time felt free-to-play; later missions ran up to three real days; operations have no failure
  state and mainly add narrative colour; changing the system clock bypasses them. [O] (MobyGames, GameBanshee,
  Co-optimus, Digital Trends via search)
- Lesson: pick game-time timers, give failure stakes, cap the number of open decisions.

**Mass Effect 3 war assets**
- Assets add to Total Military Strength; Galactic Readiness (a percentage mostly raised through multiplayer and the mobile
  app, and decaying) scaled it into Effective Military Strength (example: 550 x 50% = 275). Legendary Edition removed
  readiness. [D] (search summary of GameSpot, RPG Site)
- Lesson: when the number that decides the ending depends on something outside the story (a second system), players
  resent it. Our "market share" number should be earned only by things in the game.

**Ni no Kuni II kingdom building**
- Kingdom management is praised as "satisfying enough to be a game on its own" (GamesRadar) and "satisfying and engaging"
  (Game Informer); the system opens at Chapter 4; a reviewer admits "it's rote, it feels rote" but kept playing. [O]
  (search summaries of GamesRadar, Game Informer, GameSpot, Destructoid)
- Lesson: recruiting citizens through side missions and seeing new facilities appear is a good loop; late unlock is
  acceptable.

**Kingdom Come: Deliverance 2**
- I found no business or property system. Players on Steam ask for property buying; none confirmed. [O]
  (steamcommunity.com/app/1771300 threads via search). Nothing to learn here except that players ask for it.

**GTA V properties and stock market**
- Two exchanges: LCN (offline, reacts to your own actions) and BAWSAQ (online, driven by players). Lester's assassination
  missions move prices: killing a corporate head lifts rivals, sabotage lifts a rival, effect within about ten hours then
  stabilises, sabotage prices normalise in about two days; the market can change "in a few dozen minutes". Guides tell you
  to buy before the mission and sell after. [D] (gamepressure.com/gtav/stock-exchange-how-to-play/z355b7)
- Lesson: THE best example of "my adventure moved the market". The player plans (buy), acts (the hit), collects (sell). Our
  quests should be the Lester missions of the Mojave.

**Stardew Valley shipping bin**
- You drop goods in the bin and are paid after sleeping; you can pull back only the last item the same day; shipping fills
  the Shipping Collection and Full Shipment achievement. [D] (stardewvalleywiki.com/Shipping_Bin via search summary)
- Lesson: one daily payout moment, simple input, a collection goal behind it. The end-of-day tally is what makes "one more
  day" feel good.

### Pattern across the games

1. The systems that are loved give personality (Pearson, the majordomo, hostesses, villa shops) and visible change.
2. The systems that are hated or skipped are chore-shaped (Hearthfire hauling, FO4 repairs), have unclear rules, punish
   absence, or have no stakes (DAI "no failure").
3. The best systems are gated by a deed from the main adventure (AC Brotherhood towers, Corvo Bianco from a quest).

## 2. What this means for our mod

### (a) Twelve concrete lessons

1. **Gate each tier with a deed in the world, not only money.** AC Brotherhood: burn the tower, then renovate. Ours: clear
   Quarry Junction before the Sloan stone yard exists. [D] (theregister.com)
2. **Make every upgrade one thing you can use at once, and time it in game days.** Corvo Bianco: 1,000 crowns, 1 day, a new
   bed. Ours: "Smokehouse: 1 game day, +1 food line in Primm". [D] (gamepressure.com Corvo Bianco)
3. **Name the staff and let them speak.** Yakuza 0 hostesses, RDR2's Pearson/Strauss, Corvo Bianco's majordomo.
   [O/D] A named manager per town (a vanilla-style NPC with a face, fixed lines) is the shopfront of the company.
4. **Time away must be productive, never punished.** FO4 settlements were attacked while away and defence "doesn't work
   unless you are there". [O] (steam thread). Ours: absence only delays growth, it never destroys unless a deadline you saw
   passes.
5. **Resolve away-time in game time, never wall-clock.** DAI's real-time timers were disliked ("free-to-play"). [O]
   Our game time is already 48 real minutes per game day. Use game days.
6. **Cap open decisions.** DAI table "overwhelming" at the midpoint. [O] Ours: at most three memos per morning report,
   expire by default to a safe option.
7. **Give failure a face.** DAI ops had "no failure state" and read as colour. [O] A route that fails should name the cause
   (Powder Gangers, deathclaw, a Crimson Caravan undercut) and let you fix it by going there.
8. **Colour-code risk, and let the player lower it with an action.** Kenway's Fleet green/yellow/orange/red; a fight makes
   the route safer. [D] Ours: a route's danger rating drops when the player clears the road in person.
9. **Let the player's actions move prices, with a delay and a decay.** GTA V Lester: buy, act, sell; effects fade within
   hours to two days. [D] Ours: kill the Quarry Junction deathclaws and stone prices at Sloan fall for several game days.
10. **One daily payoff moment.** Stardew bin pays at night. [D] Ours: the morning report, once per game day.
11. **Do not require hauling.** Hearthfire "you gather your own iron", GTA Online pickup trips. [O] Ours: no pickup trips;
    adventuring can supply things (optional), never the only way.
12. **Show the change.** Brotherhood rebuilds a street; Corvo Bianco opens a hidden lab and adds visible stands and
    paintings; RDR2 adds chickens and hitching posts. [D] See (d) below.
13. **Design the money sink at the start.** FO76 added a 10% tax later and angered players. [D/O] Ours: wages, rent,
    licences and the loan are the sinks from day one, and every tier adds a bigger sink.
14. **Do not let a second, hidden number gate the ending.** ME3 readiness. [D] Our market-share number is computed only
    from visible things (stalls, routes, staff), and shown in the terminal.
15. **A copy of the last system is disliked.** RDO Naturalist vs Trader. [O] Each tier must add a new kind of decision, not
    the same thing bigger. See the tier list.

(That is 15 lessons; 1-12 are the strongest.)

### (b) Session structure

Time frame: game day = 48 real minutes (timescale 30), week about 5.6 real hours.

**5-minute visit to HQ (Goodsprings office, terminal in the back room)**
1. Walk in; the manager greets with one line that depends on state ("Cobb's boys robbed a wagon at dawn").
2. Terminal first screen: cash, runway in days, the three most important numbers as plain text (cash, this week's
   bill, market share Primm/Sloan/Outpost).
3. Memos (max 3): each a real decision with a default. Example: "The Primm smokehouse is short 40 brahmin feed. Buy at
   Mojave Outpost at 12% over, or run short and sell 30% fewer rations." Choose.
4. One action: sign one contract, hire one trader, or rest a route. Leave.
5. Result: five minutes, 2-3 decisions, nothing forced. (Stardew daily tally feel, [D].)

**30-minute session (about 0.6 game day)**
1. The 5-minute loop plus one local errand that the company gives: walk the new caravan out to the first stop and watch it
   (real caravans walk only near the player; money is ledger math).
2. Go to a town in the route (Primm). Check the stall, talk to the manager, close one contract in person (face-to-face
   deal gives a better price: lesson 9).
3. Build or upgrade one thing (1 game-day build, Corvo Bianco style).
4. Return; the terminal shows the plan result. A visible change appears (a sign, a cart).
5. A 30-minute session has one "plan, act, see result" loop.

**Return after 10 game days away (about 8 real hours of game time passing; the player was questing)**
Not a payout screen. A written report in the terminal, as a letter from the manager, in four parts:
1. **What happened** (4-6 lines of named events, computed from the ledger): "Day 3: caravan 2 reached Sloan, sold 60%
   of the stone. Day 6: Crimson Caravan undercut your water in Primm by 8%. Day 8: wagon 1 attacked near Quarry Junction;
   guards held, you lost 2 crates."
2. **Money**: income, bills paid, bills due (with date), cash. No hidden penalty: if cash would drop below zero the report
   says "I held the Sloan order back"; the ladder stage is shown.
3. **Three or four decisions that exist because of the time away** (not generic): "The Primm sheriff is gone (you killed
   him); the Powder Gangers asked to ride your route for 15% of cargo. Accept, refuse, or ask the NCR for an escort (cost
   200 caps, 3 days)." Each has a deadline in game days.
4. **One hook** that points at the world: "Sunny says the geckos are back near the spring. Your water stops in 2 days
   unless you clear them."
Rules: decisions never expire during the time you were away without having been shown first (the ladder warns before
punishing, per BUSINESS-RESEARCH.md lessons); the report is capped at one screen of text plus up to three decisions.

### (c) Main and side quests feed the company, and the company feeds the quests

Every row: the vanilla event, what the company sees, what the player gets back. I could not re-read the quest records
this session; the quest behaviours are from memory [M] and should be checked with fnv_record/fnv_quest before use.

| Vanilla quest [M] | Feeds the company | Company feeds the quest |
|---|---|---|
| Ghost Town Gunfight (Goodsprings vs Powder Gangers, Joe Cobb) | Clears Goodsprings of its main danger: HQ becomes viable; Goodsprings trust +1 tier | The office gives Sunny/Trudy an order (jobs); rumours say you hold the town |
| Run Goodsprings Run (Goodsprings aftermath) | Decides who holds Goodsprings; if the town survives, the HQ lease is cheap | A manager comment in the report when the outcome changes the town |
| My Kind of Town (Primm sheriff) | Primm becomes safe or not: the Primm stall and the smokehouse/reloading bench can open; whoever is sheriff changes taxes and escorts | A sheriff you made gives a better route-safety rating; the report names him |
| Quarry Junction deathclaws | Sloan stone yard unlocks; stone cargo possible; Sloan safe | Clearing it in person is the deed that makes tier 3 (building materials) possible |
| Powder Gangers at NCR Correctional | Their raids on the road stop (if killed), or become a contract partner (if allied) | Powder is a smuggling source (dodgy side) from them; NCR heat when you buy it |
| Mojave Outpost quests | NCR trust up or down; the Outpost is a key warehouse town | A long Outpost warehouse contract with the NCR as the government |
| Heartache by the Number (Cass, Crimson Caravan) | Alice McLafferty and the rival's history; do not break it. The loan from her has teeth because of this story | Company state alters Alice's lines only in a separate mod-owned topic, never vanilla's |

Other feeding paths:
- **Escort runs**: the memo "Cobb's gang near the road, 3 guards vs danger 5" can be taken in person (walk the road) or
  resolved on paper.
- **Rumours**: NPCs in Goodsprings/Primm mention your company by name when market share passes 20%/40%.
- **Reputation**: adventure fame sets hiring availability (a famous courier hires cheaper traders).

### (d) Growth shown in the world: 20 visible changes by tier

Tier 1 Peddler (one cart, no office)
1. A hand cart and brahmin parked behind Goodsprings store; a painted crate with the company mark.
2. A chalkboard sign by the cart listing today's goods (text changes with stock).
3. The first hired trader walks a short loop on a leash.
4. Goodsprings kids and Trudy comment: "Is that your cart, courier?"

Tier 2 Outfit (HQ rented, 2 caravans)
5. A rented building in Goodsprings gets a wooden company sign over the door.
6. A terminal and a desk with stacked crates visible through the window.
7. A manager NPC stands at the desk, shows a day/night schedule.
8. Caravan brahmin with company banners walk the road to Primm (when the player is in range).
9. Primm stall opens: a counter with a company flag.

Tier 3 Caravan company (4+ caravans, a workshop)
10. A second building: a stone yard at Sloan with piles of cut stone that grow with throughput (visible stock piles).
11. The Primm smokehouse's chimney smokes when production is above zero.
12. Goodsprings houses get new wooden extensions built from your stone (the "materials grow the towns" idea).
13. Hired guards in a company coat patrol the road for a mile either side of Goodsprings.
14. Crimson Caravan stalls shrink a little in towns where you pass 50% share (fewer crates, a "closing" sign).

Tier 4 Trading house (warehouses, many staff)
15. A warehouse with a gantry and company wagons waiting outside.
16. A rival's agent loiters near your wagons, and Alice's agents visit your HQ.
17. NCR clerk NPC in your office (inspection).
18. Street lamps and a painted road mark leading to your warehouse.

Tier 5 Mojave concern
19. Your company flag flies at Mojave Outpost and Primm.
20. A large company building with a long bench of staff walking in shifts.

Change that is never visible is not counted; a visible change appears within 1 game day of the upgrade (Corvo Bianco's
upgrades appear in 1-3 days).

### (e) What NOT to do

From the sources above:
1. Do not create upkeep that grows with success (FO4: "takes over your entire game"). [O]
2. Do not punish absence with random attacks you cannot prevent by being away. [O]
3. Do not hide the rules (FO4: attack triggers unclear). [O] The terminal shows every number that matters.
4. Do not add chores (Hearthfire hauling, GTA Online pickup). [O]
5. Do not use real-time timers (DAI). [O]
6. Do not leave decisions with no failure (DAI "no failure"). [O]
7. Do not let upgrades look the same (Hearthfire sameness). [O]
8. Do not let the number that gates progress depend on another system (ME3 readiness). [D]
9. Do not tax or sink late after players have a stash (FO76). [D/O]
10. Do not clone a system into a new tier (RDO Naturalist). [O]
11. Do not let the daily report grow into a wall of options (DAI "overwhelming"). [O]
12. Do not make a company with nothing but menus (FO4 felt "like The Sims, not an RPG"). [O]

## 3. Bold ideas

1. **"The Lester contract" market (GTA V stock idea)**: the Company keeps a visible price board of goods at Primm, Sloan,
   Outpost. Quests move it: kill the Quarry Junction deathclaws and stone at Sloan falls over ~3 game days (lesson 9). A
   terminal memo "Hold stone for 3 days; prices fall after the Outpost garrison clears the road" is a plan you act on in the
   field. How hard: medium (a price table updated by quest flags; terminal menu items refreshed).
2. **Route colour and the "clear the road" walk**: each route has a danger colour (Kenway's Fleet). The player can walk
   it with a hired guard and the rating drops one level for 10 game days. Fun: a short, optional walk gives a visible
   payoff in the next report. Hard: low (a number and a flag).
3. **The manager as a character**: one named manager per town with 8 fixed lines depending on state, one personal
   running thread (e.g. the Primm manager wants a sheriff; he thanks you if you finish My Kind of Town). Fun: Yakuza-style
   attachment. Hard: medium (writing, dialogue topics).
4. **Rumour ticker**: every morning the HQ clerk reads one rumour that is computed from the ledger ("the Crimson Caravan
   is cutting prices in Primm"); each is a hook to go. Hard: low (text templates).
5. **Letters instead of a report screen**: the return report is a letter from the manager that quotes real events; it
   reads like a story, not a statement. Hard: low-medium.
6. **Deed ledger**: the terminal lists which quest outcomes unlocked which company capabilities, so the player sees their
   adventure paid off ("Quarry Junction cleared: stone yard unlocked"). Hard: low.
7. **Monteriggioni mirror**: after a town passes a threshold, a visible new building appears (AC II/Brotherhood). Hard:
   medium (placed objects enabled by a flag; no spawning).

## 4. Pitfalls and design flaws to avoid (including in the current proposals)

- The proposed "takeover: your office becomes a Crimson Caravan branch" is strong, but must be visible and warn two
  game weeks ahead; do not do it silently while the player is away (see BUSINESS-RESEARCH.md invisible takeovers).
- "The Crimson Caravan moves in on profitable routes" can feel like FO4 attacks if it is random: make it a visible agent
  you can bribe, outbid, or fight (a decision, not a punishment).
- Ledger-only simulation can feel dead (DAI "no failure"; Fallout 4 "Sims" feel in reverse): ensure that every number has
  a place in the world and a character that speaks it.
- A "market share per town" number that rises with passive work alone is a readiness-style hidden number; tie it to
  visible things (stalls, deals closed in person).
- Production chains must not become hauling: buildings do the production; the player never carries crates.
- Memos must be cleared by the player, not left forever: each memo expires to a safe default with a visible cost.
- Public release: any loop that needs the player to be in a particular cell at a particular time breaks on other load
  orders; keep the loop in the terminal.
- Everything above marked [M] about FNV quests must be checked in game data before it becomes design.

## 5. Questions only the user can answer

1. Should the company's day report be one letter from the HQ manager (voice, characters) or a bare terminal table? I
   recommend a letter with a table below it.
2. Should clearing roads in person be required for tier gates (deeds), or only a faster alternative to money?
   I recommend required for tiers 3 and 5, optional elsewhere.
3. May the company's actions affect vanilla NPC dialogue (a mod-owned topic only), or must vanilla stay entirely
   untouched?
4. How many named managers are you prepared to write lines for (my suggestion: 3 for the first version: Goodsprings,
   Primm, Sloan)?
